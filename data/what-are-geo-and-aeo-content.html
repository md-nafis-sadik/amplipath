Key Takeaways

  • AEO (answer engine optimization) makes your content the clearest, most usable answer to a specific question.
  • GEO (generative engine optimization) shapes how your brand and pages get represented, mentioned and cited inside AI-generated answers.
  • Neither one replaces SEO. Google says it plainly: optimizing for its AI features is still SEO. Crawlable pages, useful content, clear structure and real authority.
  • The pages that earn citations add something people can check: first-hand experience, original data, sharp definitions, honest comparisons or well-sourced facts.
  • No schema type, llms.txt file or agency can guarantee a citation. You can raise your odds. The platform still makes the final call.
  • Measure citations, accurate mentions, share of voice, AI referral traffic and conversions. Rankings alone won't tell you enough.

Here's a question I'm hearing from business owners more and more: "We show up on Google. So why doesn't ChatGPT ever mention us?"

It's a fair question. And the answer usually isn't one missing trick. It comes down to whether AI systems can find your pages, understand them, and trust them enough to use them as a source.

That's what GEO and AEO are about. In this guide I'll explain what each term means, how they relate to the SEO you already know, how AI search picks its sources, and the practical steps we use at Amplipath to make pages more citation-worthy. I'll also be honest about what doesn't work, because this space is full of shortcuts that sound good and do very little.

Search Visibility Is Changing

For most of the web's history, being visible in search meant one thing: showing up in a list of links. Someone typed a few words, scanned the results and clicked. That still happens. It's just not the whole journey anymore.

Now people type (or say) full questions, and they often get a written answer back before they see a single link. That answer might compare options, pull from several sources and cite only two or three pages.

So the question changes. "Do we rank?" still matters. But you also need to ask:

  • Can an answer engine understand what we're claiming?
  • Would an AI system trust the evidence behind it?
  • When it needs a source, is our page the useful one?

It also helps to separate three outcomes that people tend to lump together:

💬

Mention

The AI names your brand somewhere in its answer.

👍

Recommendation

It presents you as a good option for the person asking.

🔗

Citation

It links or attributes part of the answer to your page.

You want all three. Citations are the most valuable, though, because they send the reader to a page they can actually visit, judge and act on.

One thing worth knowing up front: Google's own guidance says its AI features still run on core Search. It describes two techniques. Query fan-out is when the model runs several related searches at once. Retrieval-augmented generation (also called grounding) is when it pulls current pages from the Search index before writing a response (Google Search Central). In plain terms: if your site can't be crawled, indexed and understood, it has very little chance of becoming a source.

What Is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)?

Definition

Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the practice of making your content easy for search engines and AI systems to use when they answer a specific question.

AEO grew out of featured snippets, voice search and question-based queries. Today it also covers chat interfaces and AI-generated answers.

But AEO isn't "add an FAQ to every page." A useful answer has to match what the person actually wants, settle the question clearly, and carry enough context to stay accurate when it's quoted somewhere else, away from the rest of your page.

The best AEO content is direct without being thin. Answer first. Then explain the reasoning, the evidence, the exceptions and what to do next.

Here's an example. Say a page targets "How long does it take to build a mobile app?" Opening with three paragraphs about how innovative your company is wastes the reader's time. Lead with a qualified answer instead. Then explain what changes the timeline, how an MVP differs from a mature product, and how the reader can estimate their own scope. That structure helps the human first. It also hands an answer engine a clean passage it can quote.

What Is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

Definition

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving how your content and brand appear in responses written by AI systems such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews.

The goals go beyond a single link. GEO aims to earn citations, increase accurate brand mentions, tie your brand firmly to your category, and get you included when people ask for recommendations or comparisons.

The term itself comes from a research paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues, presented at KDD 2024 (Aggarwal et al.). In their benchmark, adding quotations, statistics and source citations to content made it more visible in generated answers. The best methods improved visibility by up to about 40%.

That's encouraging. It shows that how you write a page can affect whether AI uses it. What it doesn't show is that your page will get 40% more traffic or citations in live search. It was a controlled test, and the authors found that what worked changed from one subject area to another.

So GEO is bigger than rewriting paragraphs. It covers technical access, a clear and consistent brand identity, content quality, evidence, third-party validation, topic depth and ongoing measurement. A page can be beautifully written and still invisible if the site blocks the right crawler, has no internal links pointing to it, says nothing new, or contradicts what the rest of the web says about the brand.

How AI Search Engines Find and Cite Sources

Nobody outside these companies can see every rule inside their systems, and each platform chooses sources a little differently. Still, the public documentation supports a practical five-stage model.

01

Discovery

A crawler or search index has to reach the page. XML sitemaps, internal links and clean navigation help. Blocked crawlers, noindex tags, login walls and JavaScript a crawler can't run will stop it.

02

Interpretation

The system tries to understand what the page is about, which entities it mentions and whether it's current. Clear headings, descriptive titles, consistent brand details and the right structured data all reduce guesswork.

03

Retrieval

When someone asks a question, the platform pulls material that looks relevant. A complex question can trigger several related searches behind the scenes. That's why one well-built guide can show up for lots of related questions, and why you don't need a separate page for every way of wording them.

04

Evaluation and synthesis

The system compares what it found, looks for supporting evidence and writes the answer. Original data, precise explanations, reputable references and a visible author give it far more to work with than a generic summary.

05

Attribution

The platform may cite or link the pages that backed up the answer. Citations are selective and they move around. The same prompt can produce different sources on different platforms, in different locations, on different days.

The two-test rule. A page has to be retrievable and it has to be worth using. Great content that crawlers can't reach fails the first test. A perfectly crawlable page with nothing new to say fails the second.

10 Practical Ways to Become More Citation-Worthy

None of these are hacks. They're the things that make a page genuinely more useful, which is exactly why they tend to work across Google, Bing, ChatGPT and Perplexity at the same time.

1Build around real questions and real decisions

Start with what customers ask before they buy, compare, troubleshoot or call an expert. Then separate the informational questions from the commercial ones. "What is GEO?" needs a definition and an explanation. "Best GEO agency for a SaaS company" needs comparison criteria, proof and a clear sense of fit. A page that tries to serve every intent usually serves none of them well.

Build a question library from sales calls, support tickets, your site search, Search Console, customer interviews, forums and your own AI chats. Group closely related questions into one strong page instead of publishing a thin page for every keyword variation.

2Give the answer early, then earn the reader's trust

Open each important section with a direct answer in plain language. Follow it with evidence, examples, exceptions and a practical next step.

There's no magic word count. Google's guide actually notes that you don't need to split content into tiny pieces for its AI to understand it, because its systems can handle several topics on one page (Google Search Central). Microsoft's advice points the same way: descriptive headings, answers that make sense on their own, specific claims instead of vague ones, and key information in real HTML text rather than locked inside images or PDFs. It also admits there's no secret sauce (Search Engine Journal).

3Publish information that deserves to be cited

AI systems don't need another page repeating the same ten tips. Give them something distinctive they can verify: original research, benchmark data, first-hand tests, a clear methodology, expert interviews, detailed case studies, comparison tables, crisp definitions or statistics you keep up to date.

Can't run original research? You can still make existing information more useful. Show where sources agree, point out where they differ, be upfront about the limits of the evidence, and explain how it applies in real life. That's real added value, and it shows.

4Back up your claims with reputable sources

Link important claims to primary or authoritative sources whenever they exist. Use the original research paper, not a blog post that summarized it. Use official platform documentation, not someone's guess about it. Use current statistics, not undated numbers copied from another article. And only cite sources that actually support the sentence they're attached to.

The outbound link isn't the point by itself. Good sourcing makes your claims easy to check and shows readers where the facts came from. It's also one of the methods that improved visibility in the original GEO study, although results varied by subject (Aggarwal et al.).

5Show who wrote it and why they're qualified

Use a real byline, a useful author bio and an editorial process that fits the topic. An author profile should cover relevant experience, link to other work and connect to credible profiles. For high-stakes topics like finance, health and law, expert review and careful sourcing matter even more.

On blog posts, Article or BlogPosting structured data can identify the headline, author, publish date, update date and main images. Google recommends pointing the author's url to a page that uniquely identifies that person, such as a bio page or a social profile (Google Search Central). That's exactly why every Amplipath article ends with an author box, and why our About page names the people behind the work.

6Make your brand facts consistent everywhere

AI systems build a picture of your company from your own site and from the wider web. Keep your company name, description, services, locations, leadership, contact details and claims the same on your homepage, About page, social profiles, directories, press coverage and partner pages. Conflicting facts make it harder for any system to describe you accurately.

Organization structured data can help search engines understand your logo, contact details and other identity information (Google Search Central Blog). But it should describe facts that are already visible on the page. It's not a substitute for a credible About page or independent evidence. This is the core of what we do in AI Brand Positioning: making sure AI tools describe your business the way you'd describe it yourself.

7Fix technical access and indexation

Make sure your important content is in the initial server-rendered HTML, or at least reliably available to crawlers. Use descriptive URLs, canonical tags, internal links, XML sitemaps, correct status codes and mobile-friendly pages. Don't hide key answers behind clicks, logins or scripts a crawler can't run.

Crawler permissions matter more than most people realize. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links (OpenAI). Perplexity says PerplexityBot exists to surface and link websites in its results, and suggests allowing it in robots.txt if you want to appear there (Perplexity).

Training crawlers and search crawlers aren't the same thing, so review each user agent on purpose rather than applying one blanket rule. A simple robots.txt entry that allows the search crawlers looks like this:

# Allow AI search crawlers (review with your team first)
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

If you're not sure what your current setup is blocking, a technical SEO audit is the fastest way to find out.

8Use structured data accurately (it isn't magic)

Use schema that matches what's actually on the page: Organization on your homepage or About page, Article or BlogPosting on articles, BreadcrumbList for navigation, and Product, SoftwareApplication, LocalBusiness or Service where they genuinely apply. Validate it, and keep it in sync with the visible content.

Structured data can help machines understand a page and qualify it for rich results. But there's no special "AI citation schema." Google says outright that structured data isn't required for generative AI search (Google Search Central). FAQ markup can describe a real FAQ section. It won't force a rich result or a citation.

9Build topical depth and smart internal links

One lonely article rarely proves broad expertise. Build a connected set of pages that covers the topic from definitions to implementation, comparisons, measurement and troubleshooting. Then link them together with descriptive anchor text so readers and crawlers can follow the thread.

This article is a good example. It's the starting point, and it links out to deeper pages on SEO, generative engine optimization and content strategy, which link back where it makes sense.

10Earn credible signals beyond your own website

You can't create independent validation entirely on your own domain. Digital PR, relevant industry coverage, expert contributions, partner pages, reviews, reputable directories and genuine community participation all reinforce what your brand is known for.

The goal isn't to manufacture mentions. It's to make your expertise visible in the places your audience already trusts.

⟳

Bonus: keep important pages current

AI answers go wrong when their sources are out of date. Review key pages on a schedule, fix outdated claims, show a real "last updated" date and keep useful URLs stable when you update. For Bing and other participating engines, IndexNow lets you notify search engines the moment a URL is added, changed or removed. Use it alongside your XML sitemap, not instead of it.

What Does Not Guarantee AI Visibility

GEO has grown fast, and so has the market for shortcuts. Most of them mix up being eligible with being guaranteed.

Myth

"Add an llms.txt file"

Google says Google Search doesn't use llms.txt files for its generative features (Google). Other systems may. It's not a universal standard, and it doesn't replace crawlable HTML, robots.txt or sitemaps.

Myth

"FAQ schema gets you cited"

It can describe your content accurately. It can't force an AI system to pick your page or show a citation.

Myth

"Just rank #1 for one keyword"

AI systems may pull several pages through related searches, and citations change with the prompt and context.

Myth

"Write for the machine"

Keyword stuffing and awkward answer fragments make a page worse. Write for the person who has to act on the information.

Myth

"Publish hundreds of AI pages"

Google's spam policies treat mass-produced pages that add no value as scaled content abuse (Google).

Myth

"Buy mentions"

Low-quality paid placements don't create real expertise, and they can bring reputational and spam risk.

Red flag

"We guarantee you'll be cited by ChatGPT"

No ethical provider controls how a platform retrieves and cites sources. If someone promises a guaranteed ChatGPT, Gemini or AI Overview placement, walk away.

How to Measure GEO and AEO Performance

Treat AI visibility like a funnel. A citation is worth something when it builds awareness, sends qualified visitors, helps someone decide or contributes to a sale. No single tool shows the full picture, so you'll need a few sources.

Platform visibilityCitations, cited pages, grounding queries, brand mentions, competitor share of voiceWhether AI systems are picking and representing your content
Website trafficReferral sessions from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and other identifiable sourcesWhether visibility turns into visits
EngagementEngaged sessions, scroll depth, return visits, assisted journeysWhether AI-referred visitors find the page useful

AccuracyCorrect company facts, category association, claims, recommendation contextWhether your brand is described the way you intend

Start with the platform reports that now exist

Google recommends the Generative AI performance report in Search Console to see how your content performs in its generative AI features (Google Search Central). On the Microsoft side, Bing Webmaster Tools added an AI Performance report in February 2026. It shows total citations, average cited pages, a sample of grounding queries, page-level citation activity and trends over time (Microsoft Bing). In October 2026 Bing added a Topics Portfolio beta that groups those queries into broader themes (Microsoft Bing). These first-party reports should be your starting point.

Keep a consistent set of test prompts

Create a fixed list of high-value prompts covering informational, comparison, commercial and brand questions. Test them on a regular schedule and record whether your brand is mentioned, cited or recommended, which competitors show up, which URLs get used, and whether the description of your business is accurate.

Don't treat one manual test as a stable ranking. Results shift with location, personalization, model version and wording.

Connect visibility to revenue

Tag campaigns where you can, watch your referral sources and note the dates of major content updates. Then compare your cited pages against engagement and conversion data. The goal isn't a pile of vanity citations. It's knowing which topics and pages actually move profitable customers. If you want a baseline before you start, our free audit is a good place to begin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are GEO and AEO the same thing?

No, but they overlap a lot. AEO is about making your content useful as a direct answer to a specific question. GEO is broader: it's about how your content and brand get represented, mentioned and cited in AI-generated responses. Both rely on solid SEO underneath.

Does GEO replace traditional SEO?

No. Search engines and AI experiences still depend on crawlability, indexation, relevance, quality and authority. GEO extends SEO into generated answers and citation-based discovery. It doesn't replace it.

Can a page be cited by AI without ranking #1 on Google?

Yes. Generative systems can pull from several sources and run related searches in the background, so a lower-ranking page can still contribute a specific fact or explanation. That said, strong organic visibility and clean indexation give you far more chances to be picked.

Does structured data or FAQ schema guarantee an AI citation?

No. Accurate structured data helps machines understand a page and can support search features, but no schema type forces ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot or Google's AI experiences to cite you. Google says structured data isn't required for its generative AI features.

Do we need an llms.txt file?

Not for Google. Google says Google Search doesn't use llms.txt for its generative features. Other platforms may handle it differently, so treat it as optional and platform-specific, never as a replacement for technical SEO.

How long does GEO or AEO take to work?

There's no fixed timeline. Technical fixes can improve eligibility fairly quickly once pages are recrawled. Authority, topic coverage and stable citations usually take months. It depends on crawl frequency, competition, content quality and how much independent evidence exists about your brand.

Can an agency guarantee citations in ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews?

No responsible agency can. Each platform controls its own source selection, and it changes with the prompt, location, date and model. What an agency can do is improve your access, evidence, clarity, authority and measurement, which raises your odds.

What type of content is most likely to earn AI citations?

Content with a clear purpose and value people can verify: original research, expert explanations, current statistics, honest comparisons, clear definitions, case studies, documented methods, and answers that settle a specific question better than generic summaries do.

How do we know if AI search is actually bringing in business?

Look at citations and brand visibility together with AI referral traffic, engagement, qualified leads, sales and assisted conversions. A citation is most useful when it moves a real customer closer to buying.

Which AI search engine should a business optimize for first?

Start with the platforms your customers actually use and the ones already shaping your category. Google and Bing remain the foundation for discovery. ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and Copilot can matter more or less depending on your audience, country and buying journey.

Conclusion

GEO and AEO are useful names for a real shift. More and more people get a written answer before they ever visit a website. But the right response isn't to abandon SEO or chase every new trick that shows up on LinkedIn.

It's to publish information that's easy to access, precise, different from what everyone else is saying, and properly supported. Then make sure the brand behind it looks consistent and credible everywhere people (and machines) look.

If you take one thing from this guide, make it this: build pages that a knowledgeable person would trust and that a retrieval system can reach, understand and verify. That won't guarantee a citation. It does give your content a legitimate reason to be chosen.
